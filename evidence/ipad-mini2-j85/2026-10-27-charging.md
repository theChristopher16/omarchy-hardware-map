# iPad mini 2 J85 charging-source investigation

This owner-run report concerns one iPad mini 2 Wi-Fi (A1489/J85, Apple A7)
on an experimental Linux RAM boot, observed on 27 October 2026. The broader
[bring-up report](https://github.com/OrchardLinux/hardware/pull/5) records
the device context.

On an owner-confirmed Apple A1357 10 W wall adapter, a bounded sequence of
1 A input-limit trials reported positive battery current and a rise from
48% to 51% on that device. Each completed cycle reported restoration of
the prior setting. This was an observed experiment, not proof of automatic
source detection or safe operation with arbitrary chargers and USB hosts.

Later 500 mA samples with the display lit showed battery discharge. A
source label or battery-current reading cannot establish what is connected
now. A read-only inventory of one Linux device-tree I²C controller found its
PMIC child but no TriStar child on that controller. It did not conduct an
I²C transaction, cover every node, or prove the hardware absent.

An offline examination of the corresponding Apple hardware and driver
metadata identifies a possible TriStar/PMU brick-ID voltage path. A later
source trace links the matching TriStar property to a PMU sensor conversion;
the selected original conversion changes PMIC registers and does not promptly
check several start or cleanup results. It is not a passive read. The earlier
nearby brick-ID debug routine belongs to a separate dock class. Linux ownership, physical calibration, hotplug/revocation handling and safe
error recovery remain unverified; no physical source-detection test followed.
A further offline trace reconstructed a historical iOS two-sample classifier
and its software policy table, tested with synthetic inputs. Its physical
applicability is unverified; it does not identify the connected source or
justify increasing the Linux input limit. No automatic source classification or
sustained Linux charging driver is established.

After the owner reconfirmed the same 10 W wall charger, a separate 30-second
1 A check measured positive battery current and restored the previous setting;
the test module unloaded and the normal supervisor resumed. The source was
owner-confirmed, not identified by Linux. A later 500 mA cycle reported a
terminal guard error and exited after reporting its baseline restored and no
held state. The saved result cannot distinguish an input-value change from a
propagated read error; the longer charging session was not started.
A subsequent one-shot gauge probe produced a valid cached 30% battery reading and reported restored gauge-interface state, but its shell action returned an error. The gauge module and its cooperative lock were initially retained, then removed through a guarded normal unload after the cached result and idle ownership were rechecked. The one-shot record remains. The command error is unexplained, and this reading does not establish current or charging. A separate bounded gauge attempt reported 31.4 C but repeated the nonzero shell status; it stopped before voltage and current, then unloaded normally. No charging setting changed. An offline source-based model reproduced a possible cause: buffered output split the command and newline into separate driver calls, allowing the probe to finish before the newline was rejected. The actual native vector boundaries and error path were not captured, so this remains a hypothesis. A new single-write ARM64 helper passed a native /dev/null smoke test and then succeeded in a separate guarded voltage/current probe. The iPad reported 3597 mV and -857 mA at that instant, indicating discharge on the owner-confirmed wall charger. The gauge unloaded normally and its one-shot record was preserved; no charging setting changed. After the owner chose to dim the screen, a separate guarded point sample reported -722 mA, compared with -857 mA before dimming. Both readings indicate discharge; the difference alone does not establish that brightness caused it. The gauge unloaded and the distinct one-shot record remained. A later guarded battery-level observation reported 15%, down from 30%; the gauge unloaded normally, all one-shot records were preserved, and no charger setting changed. A separate five-second read-only /proc sample found approximately 0.4% aggregate CPU busy and no matches among six selected test workers. It does not attribute interrupts, wakeups or unlisted processes and does not establish the cause of the discharge. No longer charging session was started.

An offline gadget-event adapter
can observe partial invalidation hints in host tests, but cannot establish
physical source loss, USB lifecycle coverage or a safe fallback; it has not
been loaded or connected to charging control.

No recovered Apple firmware, instruction bytes, device tree, calibration,
private logs, credentials or personal device identifiers are included here.
